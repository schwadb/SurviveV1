❤️ CPR (Adult)

  1. Check scene safety and responsiveness
  2. Call for help / send someone for AED
  3. Check breathing (10 seconds max)
  4. Begin chest compressions: center of chest, 2 inches deep, 100-120/min
  5. 30 compressions, then 2 rescue breaths (head-tilt/chin-lift)
  6. Continue 30:2 cycles until help arrives or patient recovers
  7. If AED arrives: turn on, follow voice prompts, resume CPR between shocks
Push hard, push fast. Minimize interruptions. "Staying Alive" by Bee Gees = 100 BPM

🩸 Severe Bleeding Control

  1. Apply direct pressure with clean cloth — press HARD
  2. If blood soaks through, add more material on top (don't remove)
  3. Elevate injured limb above heart level if possible
  4. If bleeding doesn't stop: apply tourniquet 2-3" above wound
  5. Tighten tourniquet until bleeding stops
  6. Note the time tourniquet was applied (write on forehead or arm)
  7. Do NOT remove tourniquet — only a medical professional should

🔥 Fire Starting Methods

  • Lighter/matches: Fastest. Keep dry in waterproof container
  • Ferro rod: Scrape at 45° into fine tinder. Works when wet
  • Magnifying glass: Focus sun on char cloth or dark tinder
  • Bow drill: Friction method — hardwood spindle on softwood board
  • Battery + steel wool: Touch terminals to fine steel wool
Tinder progression: char cloth → dry grass → small twigs → pencil-thick → finger-thick → wrist-thick

🏕️ Emergency Shelter Priorities

  1. Get out of wind and rain/snow FIRST — hypothermia kills fast
  2. Insulate from ground: leaves, pine needles, branches (4+ inches)
  3. Small shelter = warmer. Just big enough to lie down in
  4. Debris hut: ridgepole against tree, ribs of branches, pile leaves 3ft thick
  5. Snow cave: dig into snowbank, sleep platform above entrance
  6. Tarp: A-frame lean-to with paracord between two trees

🧭 Navigation Without GPS

  • Sun rises East, sets West (roughly)
  • North Star: Find Big Dipper, follow pointer stars to Polaris
  • Stick shadow: Mark tip of shadow, wait 15 min, mark again. Line = E-W
  • Watch compass: Point hour hand at sun, halfway to 12 = South
  • Moss: Unreliable alone, but often thicker on north side of trees
  • Follow water downhill to find streams → rivers → civilization

🌡️ Hypothermia Recognition & Treatment

StageSignsAction
MildShivering, cold hands/feet, fumblingWarm shelter, dry clothes, warm drinks
ModerateViolent shivering, confusion, slurred speechRemove wet clothes, insulate, body heat from another person
SevereShivering stops, unconscious, weak pulseHandle gently, insulate, warm core first. No rubbing. Seek help immediately
Never give alcohol. Never rub frostbitten skin. Warm slowly — rapid warming causes shock.
